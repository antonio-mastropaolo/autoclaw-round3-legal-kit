# 1. Executive recommendation

**Disposition: Do not proceed to a purchase order on the current paper.** Proceed to a PO only if every P0 item in `conditions_of_award_public.md` is agreed in writing, in a single integrated mark-up of the MSA, DPA, SLA, and Order Form. The fourteen-day clock (Brief §6: target PO 26 November 2026) does not authorize acceptance of a P0 defect. The laboratory may not waive a P0 (Checklist §1; Brief §5 chapeau).

The paper is a private-commercial form. It is not close to the University's opening position. Several defects are independently blocking. Together they would let AgentForge train on unpublished research, cap a research-data failure at roughly three months of a $60,000 subscription, refuse a real handover, and put a Virginia public university in Delaware court on a jury waiver with an open-ended indemnity running the wrong way.

## P0 list (plain English)

Do not issue a PO unless the signed paper, including the Order Form, says all of the following:

1. **No training on CCU content.** Delete the MSA training license. Prohibit training, fine-tuning, benchmarking, and "improvement" on Customer Data, Outputs, prompts, and telemetry, whether or not the record is "personal data." The ban must survive termination.
2. **CCU owns the research.** Clean ownership of Customer Data and Outputs. The vendor's license is limited to providing the Service to CCU. Feedback is a non-exclusive license, not an assignment of faculty and student ideas.
3. **A real exit.** Not less than 90 days of continued access or equivalent managed export (prefer 180), then certified deletion. Deletion must not cut off handover. No training license survives.
4. **A real liability floor, with real carve-outs.** Vendor cap not less than the greater of 12 months' fees paid or payable or US $500,000. Carve-outs for willful misconduct / gross negligence, confidentiality, indemnities, and data-protection / security failures, including forensic, notification, and regulatory costs.
5. **Credits are not the whole remedy.** Service credits may be the exclusive remedy for ordinary downtime only. They are not the exclusive remedy for a security failure, Personal Data Breach, or loss of University data.
6. **The University does not indemnify the vendor.** Delete or recast MSA §10.1. Vendor IP indemnity must cover the Service as used for academic research, must not exclude Outputs and prompts as a class, and must not be locked to the liability cap.
7. **Public-institution terms.** Virginia law, or silence; no exclusive Wilmington, Delaware venue; no jury-trial waiver; no class-action waiver; delete the "no public-entity terms" clause. No waiver of sovereign immunity. No obligation that exceeds appropriations.
8. **FERPA-capable terms.** School-official (or other documented authorized-use) language, use and redisclosure limits, and no product-analytics subprocessor on education records or identifiable research-workspace content unless CCU approves that subprocessor in writing.
9. **United States data at rest.** Customer Data at rest in the United States. No Singapore region. Cloudflare global edge and non-U.S. support access require prior University approval and a documented transfer mechanism.
10. **Hard breach clock.** Notice without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours of the vendor becoming aware. The DPA clock controls. The SLA five-business-day / "in lieu of DPA" sentence comes out.
11. **Written security commitments and assurance.** Affirmative administrative, technical, and physical measures in the contract (not only a changeable policy). Current SOC 2 Type II and a pen-test summary not more than 12 months old, on request.
12. **No free use of CCU's name.** No name, logo, mark, laboratory, or faculty name in publicity without prior written approval of University Communications.
13. **Insurance.** CGL $1M / $2M; technology E&O and cyber, including privacy-breach response, $2M per claim; workers' compensation as required; certificates on request.
14. **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.1 AA and Section 508 as applicable to a public university's ICT; current VPAT or equivalent; accessibility failure is a service defect.

## The three contradictions that most affect this disposition

**First: the training license beats the training prohibition.** MSA §6.3(d) grants a surviving license to train, fine-tune, benchmark, and improve Provider's models and other products on prompts, Customer Data, Outputs, and telemetry. DPA §4.2 prohibits training only on Customer Personal Data. DPA §4.3 then says the MSA license governs everything that is not Customer Personal Data, including non-personal research content and Outputs. MSA §8.1 says the DPA controls only as to personal-data processing and "does not limit Provider's rights under Section 6.3." The preamble ranks the MSA above the DPA. Unpublished methods, code, and notes are therefore licensed for training unless we delete MSA §6.3(d) and rewrite DPA §4 against the full Customer Data definition. This is Brief §5.1–5.2 and Checklist §4.2 / snapshot B. It is a P0.

**Second: the exclusive-remedy stack swallows security and breach duties.** MSA §8.3 makes SLA credits the sole and exclusive remedy, and Provider's entire liability, for unavailability, degradation, support failure, "or other performance issue," including a claim "characterized as a breach of this Agreement, a breach of the DPA or of Section 8.2." SLA preamble and SLA §5.2 extend that exclusive remedy to a Security Incident or Personal Data Breach that "manifests as unavailability, degradation, or loss of access." MSA §11.3 applies the cap to unauthorized access, security incidents, data loss, data corruption, and a Personal Data Breach, and states that neither party is liable for forensic, notification, credit-monitoring, regulatory-fine, or other costs from those events. DPA §12 incorporates that cap. Checklist §6.5 / snapshot N and Brief §5.4–5.5 forbid this. On a $60,000 annual subscription the credit ceiling is 10% of one month's fee (SLA §4.2) — about US $500 for a month, about US $6,000 if every month maxes out. That is not a remedy for a research-data event. It is a P0.

**Third: deletion cuts off handover.** MSA §12.4 gives a 30-day export window, only if CCU asks in writing within those 30 days, after which Provider may delete. MSA §12.5 disclaims transition assistance, transformation, and parallel-run. DPA §11.1 deletes Customer Personal Data within 90 days, or after a 30-day export window plus up to 90 more days; DPA §11.4 says this is not a managed transition. MSA §6.3(d) and §7.2 keep training artifacts and copies retained under §6.3(d). Brief §5.3 and Checklist §8.2 / snapshot D require not less than 90 days (prefer 180) of usable access or export, then certified deletion, with DPA deletion aligned to that period. Thirty days, request-or-lose, then deletion, is the exact defect the playbook names. It is a P0.

Two further conflicts sit just under those three and will decide whether the P0s stick: subprocessor notice (MSA §8.5 after-the-fact hosting notice and "this Agreement controls" versus DPA §7.3 fifteen days / ten-day objection versus Checklist §5.4 thirty days), and breach notice (DPA §10.1 "without undue delay" with no outer limit versus SLA §5.1 five business days from Provider's *determination*, expressly "in lieu of" the DPA, versus Checklist §5.6 / snapshot K seventy-two hours from awareness). Both are mapped in the contradiction matrix below (rows C-05 and C-06).
